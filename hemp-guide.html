Hemp Program Guide · July 2026

PA Hemp Program: 0 to 1

Everything you need to go from zero knowledge to understanding how to legally participate in Pennsylvania's Industrial Hemp Program. Covers the laws, permits, application process, deadlines, and what changes in November 2026.

1. Core Laws & Framework

  • Primary PA law: Pennsylvania's Industrial Hemp Program operates under the Controlled Plants and Noxious Weeds Act (3 Pa.C.S. §1501 et seq.) and the PA Hemp General Permit issued by the PA Department of Agriculture (PDA).
  • Federal basis: The 2018 Farm Bill (7 U.S.C. §1639o) legalized hemp with ≤0.3% Delta-9 THC by dry weight. Pennsylvania's program is USDA-approved.
  • Key change coming: November 12, 2026 — H.R. 5371 §781 shifts the federal definition to a total THC standard (including THCA) and imposes a 0.4 mg per-container limit on finished products. See the Laws page for details.

4. How to Get Started — Step by Step

  1. Read the rules first. Download the PA Hemp General Permit Guidelines (PDF) and Application Instructions from the PDA Hemp Program page.
  2. Choose your permit type. Growing, Processing, or Research. Each has different requirements and restrictions.
  3. Prepare your application.
    • FBI background check via IdentoGO for the applicant + key participants (within 60 days of application)
    • Proof of property control (ownership deed, lease agreement, or access agreement)
    • Site map with GPS coordinates of all growing/handling locations
    • Variety list — check approved and prohibited varieties
  4. Submit your application. Online via the PA Plants portal or by mail. Fees: $150 new / $50 renewal per location. Applications for the next growing season open October 1st on a rolling basis.
  5. Key deadline: Outdoor planting recommended by approximately April 1st. Permits expire December 31st each year and must be renewed annually.

5. Ongoing Requirements

  • Planting report: File within 10 days of planting
  • Compliance testing: A certified sampling agent must collect samples for THC analysis before harvest
  • Harvest report: File by November 15th
  • Destruction of non-compliant crops: Any crop exceeding the THC threshold must be destroyed under PDA supervision
  • Record-keeping: Maintain records of planting, testing, harvest, sales, and disposal for at least 3 years
  • Inspections: PDA may conduct site inspections at any time
  • Annual renewal: Permits expire December 31st — renew early to avoid gaps

6. Important Restrictions

  • No hemp cultivation within 3 miles of an existing medical marijuana grower/processor without special PDA approval
  • No residential grows within 200 feet of a residential structure without approval
  • Minimum planting sizes apply for commercial growing permits
  • Research permit material cannot be sold — research crops must be destroyed or disposed of after the study
  • Individuals with felony drug convictions within the past 10 years may be restricted from holding a permit

8. Post-November 2026: What Changes

H.R. 5371 §781 takes effect November 12, 2026. Here's what it means for hemp program participants:

  • Growers: THC compliance testing switches from Delta-9-only to total THC (including THCA). High-THCA hemp varieties face greater compliance risk. Select genetics with naturally low total THC.
  • Processors: The 0.4 mg per-container cap eliminates most current intoxicating hemp product lines (Delta-8 gummies, THCA concentrates, high-potency tinctures). Non-intoxicating products (topical CBD, fiber, grain) are unaffected.
  • Retailers: THCA flower and Delta-8 products lose federal hemp protection. Retailers should plan their transition strategy now.
  • Industrial hemp: Fiber, grain, and non-cannabinoid uses are explicitly preserved under the new definition.
  • SB 49 failed (June 2026) — PA has not aligned state law with the federal changes, creating a federal-state enforcement gap.
