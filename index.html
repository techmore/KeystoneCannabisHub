Building Pennsylvania's Cannabis Education, Research & Social Impact Venture

This is both an information hub and a venture. We are assembling the complete picture of Pennsylvania's cannabis ecosystem — the law, the key players, the science, and the opportunities — and building a vertically integrated greenhouse education, clone production, and social-impact employment operation grounded in it.

What This Is

The Keystone Cannabis Hub serves two purposes that reinforce each other:

Resource

A Comprehensive Information Hub

The most complete, publicly available reference on Pennsylvania's cannabis landscape — covering the legal framework, all 10 Clinical Registrants, Delaware Valley University's research program, the science of plant DNA analysis, education pathways, and social equity frameworks.

Venture

A Vertically Integrated Operation

A greenhouse-based education, clone production, breeding, and processing operation in Bucks County, PA — partnered with a university for hands-on student training and retailers for distribution, with a core mission of employing people with disabilities and out-of-work individuals at scale.

The Gap We're Filling

Pennsylvania has a $2B+ medical marijuana program, a growing hemp industry, world-class research universities, and a stated commitment to social equity — but these pieces are isolated:

  • No centralized resource connects the Clinical Registrants, their ACRC research partners, and the regulatory framework
  • Academic programs like cannabis certificates offered at several PA universities lack commercial greenhouse partners for real-world student training
  • Hemp permit entry is low-cost ($150) and accessible, but the pathway from hemp to a vertically integrated operation is poorly documented
  • Social equity goals (disability employment, workforce re-entry) are not being systematically connected to cannabis agriculture
  • A Clinical Registrant partnership — the highest-value research and commercial license — has no clear on-ramp for new collaborators

This hub closes all of those gaps in one place.

Our Model

The venture side is a vertical integration that turns education into production, and production into social impact:

PA Universities (education & research partnerships)
↓
Greenhouse / Clone Operation (hands-on training, breeding, production)
↓
Processing (extraction, formulation, packaging)
↓
Retail Partners (compliant distribution channels)
↓
Social Impact Employment (people with disabilities, workforce re-entry)

Each step in the chain creates learning opportunities for students, employment for target populations, and revenue through product sales. The information hub documents every piece so others can understand, replicate, or contribute.

Key Players

The people and institutions that make up Pennsylvania's cannabis ecosystem — and where this project fits.

Academic Partner

University Collaboration

PA universities with cannabis programs — including Jefferson (MS), Wilkes (BS), York (BS), P.I.T. (AAS), Central Penn (BA), Del Val (BS/Cert), PennWest (Cert), HACC (Cert), and others — offer a range of degree and certificate programs. A work-study partnership with any of these institutions could bridge the gap between classroom theory and commercial greenhouse experience. Full university directory →

Retail

Compliant Distribution Channels

Farm Bill-compliant retailers with third-party lab testing and education-first approaches provide a pathway for product distribution without requiring medical marijuana dispensary licensing. The focus is on building supply relationships with established, compliant businesses.

Regulator (Medical)

PA Department of Health

Administers the Medical Marijuana Program. Manages grower/processor, dispensary, and Clinical Registrant permits. All CR slots (10) currently filled. No open application windows as of mid-2026. Regulatory details →

Regulator (Hemp)

PA Department of Agriculture

Administers the Hemp Program. $150 permit fee, background check required, location restrictions apply. The accessible entry point for greenhouse education and clone production operations. Regulatory details →

Where This Is Going

A phased approach from hemp pilot to full-scale vertical integration.

Phase 1 (Now — Year 1)

Hemp Pilot

  • Secure PA hemp permit ($150)
  • Establish small greenhouse (5,000–10,000 sq ft) in Bucks County
  • Launch university work-study pilot program
  • Begin clone production for wholesale
  • Supply retail partners with flower and clones
  • 5–10 employees, incl. disability/wraparound hires
Phase 2 (Year 2–3)

Established Operation

  • Scale to 20,000+ sq ft greenhouse + processing
  • Full integration with university certificate programs
  • Active breeding program with change-log documentation
  • DNA analysis capability (Oxford Nanopore MinION)
  • 20–30 employees, majority disability/wraparound
  • Multiple retail distribution relationships
Phase 3 (Year 4–5+)

Scale & Medical Pathway

  • 100,000+ sq ft multi-greenhouse facility
  • 50–100+ employees, disability/re-entry priority
  • Full vertical integration: clone to retail
  • CR partnership or medical permit if licensing opens
  • National demonstration site for inclusive ag employment
  • This hub becomes the definitive PA cannabis reference
