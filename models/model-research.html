Research Contracting (ACRC Grants & Studies)

PA's medical marijuana law requires CRs to fund research through Academic Clinical Research Center (ACRC) partnerships with PA medical schools. This creates a dedicated funding stream for clinical cannabis research — and opportunities for researchers, labs, and clinical trial sites to participate.

Executive Summary

Pennsylvania's medical marijuana law includes a unique research mandate: every CR must maintain a research partnership with an Academic Clinical Research Center. ACRCs are PA medical schools that conduct clinical research on medical marijuana's safety and efficacy. The CRs fund these studies as a condition of their permit.

This creates a stable funding stream for clinical cannabis research that doesn't rely on federal grants (which are limited due to marijuana's Schedule I status). While the total funding per CR varies, the mandated research relationship ensures that PA generates more clinical data on medical marijuana than most states.

Key insight: This model is not accessible to individual entrepreneurs — it requires institutional affiliation with an ACRC or direct contracting with a CR. However, it funds research that benefits the entire industry, and the findings from ACRC studies inform clinical practice, product development, and regulatory policy.

Research Focus Areas

Current and emerging research areas in PA's ACRC network (see also Science page):

  • Opioid use disorder: Can medical marijuana reduce opioid consumption and overdose risk? Multiple active PA studies.
  • Chronic pain management: Comparing cannabis to traditional pain treatments for efficacy and safety.
  • PTSD and anxiety: Cannabinoid-based treatment protocols for trauma and anxiety disorders.
  • Pediatric epilepsy: CBD-dominant treatment protocols for seizure disorders.
  • Cancer supportive care: Managing chemotherapy side effects (nausea, pain, appetite loss).
  • Plant science & genetics: DNA analysis, chemotype prediction, disease resistance breeding.

Operations Blueprint

How to Participate

  1. Academic pathway: Secure a faculty position at an ACRC institution. Develop a research protocol aligned with the ACRC's focus areas and the partnering CR's interests.
  2. Clinical trial site pathway: If you operate a clinical trial site (hospital, clinic, research organization), contract with an ACRC or directly with a CR to conduct approved studies.
  3. Industry research pathway: Work for a CR or cannabis product manufacturer that funds its own research. These studies are more applied (product development, formulation testing) than academic studies.
  4. Grant-funded independent research: Apply for NIH, state, or foundation grants for cannabis research that doesn't require CR involvement (e.g., hemp/CBD studies, epidemiological research using PA's patient registry data).

Key SOPs

  • IRB protocol development and approval
  • Patient informed consent for cannabis studies
  • Investigational product handling and chain of custody
  • Data collection, management, and analysis
  • Adverse event reporting
  • Study closeout and publication

Integration with Hub Goals

Research contracting is the direct bridge between the business models library and the Science page. It also connects to:

  • Education (Model #9): ACRC research findings feed directly into university cannabis curricula at Jefferson (MS program), Penn State, and others.
  • Clinical Registrants (Model #1): Every CR's ACRC partnership is a mandated research obligation.
  • LMS modules: Research findings update the scientific content in Analytics, Botany, and Regulatory Affairs modules.
  • Social Impact: Research on medical marijuana's efficacy for underserved conditions (chronic pain, opioid use disorder) directly affects patient access and health equity.

Risks

  1. Schedule I barriers: Federal Schedule I status limits research: only one DEA-registered cannabis supply source (University of Mississippi), complex DEA registration for researchers, and limited federal grant funding.
  2. Publication constraints: CR-funded research may have publication restrictions or industry-influenced study design.
  3. Funding uncertainty: CR funding is tied to permit holders' profitability. A CR experiencing financial difficulty may reduce research spending to the minimum required by law.
  4. Recruitment challenges: Enrolling patients in cannabis studies faces stigma, logistical barriers, and competition from the legal medical program (patients can just visit a dispensary instead of joining a study).

Ready to Explore This Model?

Research contracting is the most specialized path in PA cannabis — but the findings generated through ACRC partnerships benefit the entire industry. If you're a researcher, explore the ACRC network and CR research priorities. If you're a student, the best entry path is through PA's university cannabis programs (Jefferson, Penn State, Drexel) that are directly engaged in ACRC research.
