Pennsylvania Cannabis Law & Regulation

Pennsylvania operates two parallel regulatory systems for cannabis. Understanding both is essential for any research, education, or commercial initiative.

Overview: Two Parallel Systems

Pennsylvania distinguishes between two regulatory frameworks depending on THC content and intended use. Each is administered by a different state agency with separate permitting, compliance, and enforcement structures.

Key Distinction

Hemp (cannabis with ≤0.3% Delta-9 THC by dry weight) is regulated by the PA Department of Agriculture (PDA) under the state's Hemp Program, authorized by the 2018 Farm Bill.

Medical Marijuana (cannabis with >0.3% THC) is regulated by the PA Department of Health (DOH) under the Medical Marijuana Act of 2016.

1. The Hemp Program (PA Department of Agriculture)

The hemp program is significantly more accessible for new entrants. It permits the cultivation, processing, and sale of hemp and hemp-derived products.

Permit Requirements

  • Application fee: $150 for a new permit (renewed annually)
  • Background check required for all applicants
  • Must provide GPS coordinates of growing locations
  • Minimum planting requirements may apply
  • Compliance testing for THC levels required before harvest

Location Restrictions

  • Hemp cultivation sites cannot be within 3 miles of an existing medical marijuana grower/processor without special approval
  • Distance restrictions from schools and residential areas may apply depending on local zoning

Key Advantage for Education

Hemp permits are ideal for educational greenhouse operations, clone production, breeding research, and student training because they avoid the stringent security and capital requirements of the medical program. Low-THC varieties can be handled safely in academic settings.

2. The Medical Marijuana Program (PA Department of Health)

Enacted by the Medical Marijuana Act (Act 16 of 2016), Pennsylvania's program is one of the most tightly regulated in the country. It includes three permit types:

Application Windows

The DOH has run multiple application phases (most recently Phase V in 2021 and Phase VI in 2024). As of mid-2026, no new application windows are open for grower/processor, dispensary, or Clinical Registrant permits. New entry is only possible through acquisition or transfer of existing permits under Act 63 rules.

3. Clinical Registrants (CRs)

Clinical Registrants are a unique PA category — entities that hold both a grower/processor permit and a dispensary permit, coupled with an Academic Clinical Research Center (ACRC) partnership to conduct medical marijuana research.

Statutory cap: 10 CRs. All slots are currently filled.

Perks: Each CR can operate up to 6 dispensary locations (with limits per region/county).

Research requirement: CRs must actively conduct research with their partnered ACRC (a medical school at a Pennsylvania university).

See the full Clinical Registrants page for profiles of all 10 CRs and their ACRC partners.

4. Act 63 (2024) — Recent Changes

Pennsylvania's Act 63 updated several aspects of the medical marijuana program:

  • Expanded patient access and caregiver provisions
  • Modified permit transfer and acquisition rules
  • Created pathways for existing permit holders to modify operations
  • Did not increase the CR cap of 10

5. Local Zoning Considerations

In Bucks County / Newtown / Doylestown area (relevant to a proposed greenhouse operation):

  • Agricultural/horticultural uses (including commercial nurseries and greenhouses) are generally permitted or conditionally permitted
  • Medical marijuana grower/processor uses require specific local approvals in many township ordinances
  • Always verify exact zoning with the relevant township for any proposed grow site

6. Social Equity & Diversity Requirements

PA's medical marijuana program requires diversity plans from applicants. Social-impact hiring (people with disabilities, workforce re-entry) strengthens applications and is explicitly aligned with:

  • PA Employment First policy
  • Office of Vocational Rehabilitation (OVR) programs
  • InVEST project initiatives
  • PA Center for Plant Excellence workforce goals

7. Compliance Essentials

Any cannabis operation in Pennsylvania must prioritize:

  • Seed-to-sale tracking (mandatory for medical; recommended for hemp)
  • Third-party lab testing for potency, contaminants, and pesticides
  • Security (video surveillance, access control, inventory controls)
  • Traceability — documented chain of custody for all plant material
