Executive Summary

Hemp cultivation under PA's Industrial Hemp Program is the most accessible plant-touching cannabis business model in the state. An annual PDA permit (≈$500–$1,000) and GPS coordinates are all that stand between you and a legal hemp crop.

However, accessibility comes with market risk. National hemp oversupply crashed CBD biomass prices from $4+/% CBD in 2019 to under $0.50/% CBD by 2024. Success today requires a differentiated strategy: premium indoor/greenhouse flower for smokable products, specialty cannabinoids (CBG, CBC, THCV), organic certification, grain/fiber for developing industrial markets, or vertical integration into processing and retail.

This model is central to our proposed greenhouse social enterprise: a vertically integrated hemp operation that augments university cannabis education with hands-on commercial experience and employs people with disabilities at scale.

Regulatory Deep-Dive

PA Industrial Hemp Program

Governing law: PA Industrial Hemp Act (3 Pa.C.S. §701), incorporating the federal definition at 7 U.S.C. §1639o.

Agency: Pennsylvania Department of Agriculture (PDA), Bureau of Plant Industry.

Permit type: Annual Industrial Hemp Permit. Covers cultivation, handling, and transportation. 2026 data: 115 growing permits issued (including 11 research). Applications for 2027 open October 1st.

Application fee: Approximately $500–$1,000 depending on acreage and whether you're a new or renewal applicant. Apply through PA Plants portal.

Key requirements:

  • GPS coordinates of all field locations
  • Variety registration (must use approved certified seed or approved genetics)
  • THC compliance testing (current): crops must test ≤0.3% Delta-9 THC by dry weight per 7 U.S.C. §1639o
  • THC compliance testing (Nov 12, 2026+): switches to total THC (including THCA) per H.R. 5371 §781. High-THCA varieties will face heightened compliance risk.
  • Destruction orders for any crop exceeding the threshold
  • Annual reporting on acreage, yield, and disposal

Timeline: Permits issued annually. Apply in early spring for same-year planting. Sampling and testing occurs mid-to-late season. Harvest typically August–October.

Criminal history restriction: Individuals with felony drug convictions within the past 10 years may be restricted from permit holding, though PA's program is less restrictive than the medical marijuana program on this front.

See the full legal framework and PDA Hemp Program for current forms and fees. Also see Section 7: Federal Hemp Definition Change for the November 2026 transition.

Break-Even Analysis (Premium Flower Model)

Assuming greenhouse production at 1 acre scale: $40K total investment (structure, inputs, labor, compliance) requires $80K in revenue at 50% margin. At $100/lb wholesale, that's 800 lbs — achievable with good genetics and management. At retail through a Farm Bill storefront, margins improve significantly (3–5x wholesale).

Equipment & Infrastructure

  • Land: 1–100+ acres. Well-drained soil, full sun, access to water. PA's climate is well-suited to hemp.
  • Tillage & Planting: Standard farm equipment (tractor, disc, planter) or custom hire.
  • Irrigation: Drip or overhead. Essential for consistent quality, especially in PA's variable summer rainfall.
  • Drying facility: Barn, pole building, or hoophouse for curing. Critical for flower quality.
  • Testing: Third-party lab for potency and contaminant analysis ($50–$200 per sample).

SOPs Needed

Standard Operating Procedures that every hemp farm should document (maps to ISO 8.5.1):

  • Site selection and soil preparation
  • Planting and transplanting protocol
  • Irrigation and fertigation schedule
  • Pest scouting and IPM intervention thresholds
  • THC compliance sampling procedure
  • Harvest timing and method
  • Drying, curing, and storage conditions
  • Lot tracking and chain of custody

See Botany & Cultivation and Supply Chain & Logistics LMS modules for foundational knowledge.

Social Impact

Hemp cultivation is uniquely suited to social enterprise for several reasons:

  • Accessible entry: Low capital requirement and simple permitting make it feasible for nonprofits, cooperatives, and community organizations.
  • Farm-based employment: Seasonal and year-round roles that can accommodate workers with disabilities through task segmentation (transplanting, scouting, hand-harvesting, trimming).
  • Regenerative agriculture: Hemp improves soil health, sequesters carbon, and can be grown organically — aligning with environmental justice goals.
  • Educational integration: Working hemp farms can serve as outdoor classrooms for vocational training programs.

Our proposed model combines hemp cultivation with disability employment (see Social Impact page) to create a commercially viable social enterprise.

Key Risks

  1. Price volatility: Biomass prices have collapsed 80%+ since 2019. No price supports or insurance programs exist for hemp.
  2. THC compliance: A crop exceeding 0.3% THC must be destroyed. Weather, genetics, and harvest timing all affect THC levels.
  3. Regulatory uncertainty: Farm Bill reauthorization, FDA regulation of CBD, and PA state-level restrictions on intoxicating hemp derivatives are all in flux.
  4. Lack of processing infrastructure: PA has limited hemp decortication and fiber processing facilities, making grain and fiber models difficult.
