Clinical Registrant #10

Additional & Rotating CRs

The CR landscape shifts over time through acquisitions, permit transfers, and new designations. This page covers Geisinger partnerships and the evolving CR landscape.

Overview

The Pennsylvania Department of Health maintains a statutory cap of 10 Clinical Registrant permits. While the core group of CRs has been relatively stable, the landscape can shift due to acquisitions (e.g., Cresco's acquisition of Laurel Harvest Labs), corporate restructuring, permit transfers, or revocations. This page provides context on the rotating nature of CR assignments and partners not covered in detail elsewhere.

Geisinger's Role in the CR Program

Geisinger Health System

Geisinger is one of Pennsylvania's largest and most innovative health systems, with a strong focus on data-driven population health management. Through Geisinger Commonwealth School of Medicine, the system serves as a co-ACRC partner for CR #8 (Goodblend). Geisinger's extensive electronic health records system, covering hundreds of thousands of patients across central and northeastern Pennsylvania, provides a powerful research infrastructure for studying cannabis outcomes at the population level.

How CR Permits Change

  • Acquisitions: A CR permit can be acquired as part of a corporate merger or acquisition, pending DOH approval. The acquiring company assumes the CR obligations, including the ACRC research agreement.
  • Permit transfers: CR permits may be transferred between entities with DOH approval, though the process is rigorous and requires maintaining the ACRC partnership.
  • Revocations: The DOH can revoke a CR permit for non-compliance, regulatory violations, or failure to maintain the ACRC research partnership.
  • New application windows: The DOH only opens new CR application windows when slots become available. All 10 slots have been continuously filled since the program's initial application phases.

Historical CR Changes

The most significant change to date has been the acquisition of Laurel Harvest Labs (CR #4) by Cresco Labs in 2021. Other CRs have undergone branding changes and corporate restructurings while maintaining their core CR permits and ACRC partnerships. The program has not seen a revocation or new CR designation since the initial allocation.

CR Program Structure

Each CR holds both a grower/processor permit and a dispensary permit, allowing vertical integration. CRs are permitted up to 6 dispensary locations each. The ACRC partnership requires a formal research agreement with a Pennsylvania medical school, and the CR must actively participate in cannabis research as a condition of the permit.

Future Outlook

As of 2026, all 10 CR slots are filled. Any change in the CR landscape would require a slot to open through revocation, surrender, or regulatory action. Proposed federal rescheduling or state-level adult-use legalization could reshape the program, but no immediate changes are expected to the CR structure.
