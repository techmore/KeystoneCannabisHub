About the Project

Mission, Key Players & Structure

What this project is, who the key people and institutions are, how it's structured, and answers to common questions.

Mission & Vision

Mission

To build Pennsylvania's most comprehensive cannabis research, education, and production ecosystem — connecting academic training, commercial cultivation, scientific innovation, and social impact employment into a single vertically integrated model that others can learn from and contribute to.

Vision

A Pennsylvania where cannabis education is hands-on and career-connected, where people with disabilities and workforce re-entry individuals have meaningful agricultural careers, where every Clinical Registrant's research is publicly documented, and where the regulatory pathway from hemp to medical is clearly mapped for anyone to follow.

Core Values

  • Open information: All research, data, and plans are publicly available. No gatekeeping.
  • Compliance-first: Full alignment with PA DOH, PDA, and federal regulations at every step.
  • Social impact by design: Employment for people with disabilities is not an add-on — it's built into the business model from day one.
  • Education integration: Every commercial activity creates a learning opportunity for students.
  • Scientific rigor: DNA-based verification, change-log breeding documentation, and data-driven decision making.

Proposed Business Structure

The venture side of this project requires a legal structure that balances commercial viability, regulatory compliance, and social mission. The recommended approach:

Primary Entity: Pennsylvania LLC

  • Flexible, pass-through taxation
  • Well-understood structure for hemp/agriculture operations in PA
  • Can hold hemp permits, own inventory, employ staff, and enter contracts

Consider: Benefit Corporation (B-Corp)

  • Legally embeds the social mission (disability employment, education access) into the corporate charter
  • Protects mission if ownership changes or investors join
  • Strong differentiator for grant applications, PR, and license applications

Split Structure (Alternative)

  • For-profit LLC for greenhouse, clone production, and commercial sales
  • Non-profit / 501(c)(3) for education, work-study program, and workforce development (can receive grants and tax-deductible donations)
  • Requires careful legal structuring to maintain separation while enabling collaboration

IP & Partnership Agreements

  • Any breeding IP or proprietary protocols developed with a university partner would need a clear MOU defining ownership, revenue sharing, and publication rights
  • Supply agreements with retail partners should specify quality standards, testing requirements, and terms

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this a real business or just a research project?

Both. The information hub is a public resource. The venture side (greenhouse, clone production, processing) is intended to be a real, revenue-generating operation with a social mission.

Are you affiliated with Delaware Valley University?

Not yet. We are researching and documenting their program as a potential partner. The website presents the case for why a partnership makes sense — formal discussions are a next step.

Do you have active retail partners?

Not yet. Identifying compliant Farm Bill retailers in the Bucks County area for supply relationships is a priority. Outreach will begin once the greenhouse operation is closer to production.

Can you actually get a Clinical Registrant permit?

Not currently. The statutory cap of 10 is filled, and no application window is open. The realistic path is hemp-first, with a potential CR relationship through subcontracting or future legislative expansion.

How is this funded?

This is TBD. Options include self-funding, grants (USDA hemp research, PA workforce development, disability employment incentives), impact investment, and revenue from initial clone/product sales.

Is this legal?

Yes. Hemp (≤0.3% Delta-9 THC) is legal under federal and Pennsylvania law with a PDA permit ($150). All operations would be fully compliant with applicable regulations. Medical marijuana activities would require appropriate permits not currently held.

Where will the greenhouse be located?

Bucks County, near Newtown/Doylestown. Exact zoning verification is an active priority. Agricultural/horticultural uses are generally permitted in the area.

How can I trust the information on this site?

All information is sourced from public records, official state publications, academic journals, and other verifiable sources. We cite sources where possible. We welcome corrections — this is a living document.
